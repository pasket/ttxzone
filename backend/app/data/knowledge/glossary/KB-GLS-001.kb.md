# Tabletop exercise

## Definition

A tabletop exercise (TTX) gathers the people who would handle a real incident around a scenario that unfolds in stages through injects. Participants describe what they would do, decide and communicate; nothing is executed on production systems. Its value lies in exposing gaps in plans, roles, information flows and decisions in a safe setting, and in producing an improvement plan.

## In an exercise

The planning team writes the scenario and the master list of injects, sets measurable objectives from a needs analysis, and prepares evaluation guides. During the session a Controller delivers the injects, a Facilitator keeps the discussion on the objectives, Evaluators record what they observe, and Observers watch. The session ends with a hot wash and later an after-action report.

## Common confusions

It is not a technical drill or a red-team test: participants do not run commands or restore systems. It is not a training lecture either: the scenario asks for decisions. A functional exercise, by contrast, has participants carry out actions in a simulated environment, and a full-scale exercise mobilizes real resources.
