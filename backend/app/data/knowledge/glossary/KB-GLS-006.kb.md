# Hot wash

## Definition

The hot wash is a debrief of fifteen to forty-five minutes at the end of the session, led by the Facilitator, where players and the control team say what went well, what did not and what should change. It captures impressions before people leave, and it often surfaces issues that evaluators could not see from outside the discussion.

## In an exercise

Keep a fixed slot for it in the agenda even when the scenario runs late, and record the points made, grouped by objective where possible. The notes, together with the evaluation guides and the participant feedback forms, are the raw material for the after-action report.

## Common confusions

The hot wash is not the after-action report, which comes later and is written; and it is not a place to assign blame or to rate individuals. A later, more structured meeting with the planning team to agree findings is sometimes called a debrief.
