# UK NIS Regulations and the Cyber Assessment Framework: response planning and 72-hour reporting

## Scope

Operators of essential services in energy, transport, health, drinking water and digital infrastructure in the UK, designated by thresholds, and relevant digital service providers (online marketplaces, search engines, cloud services). Each sector has a competent authority that supervises, often using the Cyber Assessment Framework as the yardstick.

## What it expects from exercises

The Cyber Assessment Framework's objective D (minimising the impact of incidents) includes principle D1, response and recovery planning: a documented plan, its use in practice, and testing and exercising based on realistic scenarios with lessons fed back. An exercise with clear findings and an action list is the direct evidence for D1.

## Notification duties

Regulation 11: notify the competent authority without undue delay and no later than 72 hours after becoming aware of an incident with a significant impact on the continuity of the essential service, with the information the authority sets out (operator, time, duration, nature and impact). Digital service providers have their own thresholds. Personal data breaches go separately to the data protection authority.

## Evidence to keep

The incident response and recovery plan, records of exercises with scenarios and participants, the lessons-learned actions and their closure, and the self-assessment against the framework. A decision log showing when awareness occurred and when the report would have gone is valuable.

## Exercise hooks

Start the 72-hour clock at a clearly marked moment, and add an inject from the competent authority asking for an update before the table has decided anything. Combine with a personal data angle so both reports are discussed. Map objectives to UK-NIS-11 and UK-NIS-CAF-D1.
