# US SEC cybersecurity disclosure: materiality decisions and four-business-day filing

## Scope

Companies that file reports with the US securities regulator, including foreign private issuers through their own forms. The rule is about disclosure to investors, not about security controls, so it reaches the board and legal counsel more than the technical team.

## What it expects from exercises

Regulation S-K Item 106 asks for an annual description of processes to assess and manage material cybersecurity risks, management's role and the board's oversight. Exercising the materiality decision, with the disclosure committee and counsel at the table, is the most common way to show those processes work.

## Notification duties

Form 8-K Item 1.05: disclose a cybersecurity incident determined to be material within four business days of that determination, describing its nature, scope, timing and material impact or reasonably likely impact; the determination itself must be made without unreasonable delay after discovery. A delay is possible only when national security or public safety officials request it in writing. Information not available at filing is added by amendment.

## Evidence to keep

The disclosure committee's charter and minutes, the materiality assessment with the facts and reasoning, the escalation path from security to the committee, and board briefings. From an exercise, the timing between discovery, escalation and determination is the point to measure.

## Exercise hooks

Give the table conflicting signals about impact (operations partly restored, data theft confirmed later, a short-seller post) and make it decide materiality on the clock. Have an inject from investor relations asking what to say on an earnings call. Map objectives to SEC-8K-105 and SEC-SK-106.
