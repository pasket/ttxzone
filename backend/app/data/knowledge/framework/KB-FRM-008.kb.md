# UK operational resilience rules: important business services and impact tolerances

## Scope

Banks, building societies, insurers, investment firms, payment and e-money firms and other firms under the UK financial regulators' operational resilience rules, set out for conduct in the senior management arrangements sourcebook. The approach is outcome-based: what matters is whether customers and markets are harmed, not which system failed.

## What it expects from exercises

Firms identify important business services, set an impact tolerance for each (the maximum disruption before intolerable harm), map the people, processes, technology, facilities and third parties behind them, and carry out scenario testing against severe but plausible disruptions, including cyber attacks. A tabletop that times a disruption against the tolerance and records whether the firm stays within it is a recognized form of scenario testing, documented in the annual self-assessment approved by the board.

## Notification duties

Operational incidents are reported to the regulators under their own notification rules and principles of openness; the regulators have also consulted on more structured incident and third-party reporting. In an exercise, the question is when the firm tells its supervisor and its customers that a service is disrupted, and what it says about restoration.

## Evidence to keep

The list of important business services with impact tolerances, the mapping, scenario test reports with findings and remediation, and the self-assessment document reviewed by the board. Supervisors look for evidence that testing changed something.

## Exercise hooks

Put the impact tolerance on the wall and run the clock against it: a payments outage caused by ransomware at a shared provider, with customer complaints growing. Ask the table to decide workarounds, customer communication and when to tell the supervisor. Map objectives to UK-OPRES-SYSC-15A.
