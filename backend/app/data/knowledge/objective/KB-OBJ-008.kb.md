# Assign incident roles and keep a regular situation report

## Objective

Within the first hour, each participating unit names who acts for it and the incident manager shares one list of roles and contacts; from then on, a situation report is issued at an agreed interval with status, actions under way, decisions needed and the time of the next update.

## Success criterion

Each participating unit names a representative; one list of roles and contacts is shared; nobody holds two roles that conflict; an update interval is agreed; each situation report states status, actions, decisions needed and the next update; no report is late by more than fifteen minutes of scenario time.

## Evidence

The roles and contacts list and the situation reports with their times. A Controller can ask for the current situation report at any time; whether the table can produce it is the measure.

## Regulatory mapping

Evidences the coordination and communication activities of an incident management process (ISO27035-2) and the response and recovery planning principle of the UK Cyber Assessment Framework, including roles and communication (UK-NIS-CAF-D1).

## Common pitfalls

Everybody in every meeting and nobody accountable; situation reports that are long narratives rather than status, actions and decisions; and the legal or communications teams joining late without a briefing. A one-page report template fixes most of it.
