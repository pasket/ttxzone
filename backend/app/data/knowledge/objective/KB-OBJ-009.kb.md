# Engage a compromised supplier and decide on cutting its access

## Objective

Once a supplier is suspected as the entry point or is itself affected, the team reaches its security contact through the agreed channel, requests the information the contract entitles it to, reviews the supplier's access and decides, weighing the operational impact, whether to suspend its connections and accounts.

## Success criterion

The supplier's security contact is found in the contract or the supplier register; the request lists the information needed and a response time; the supplier's connections and accounts are listed; the operational impact of cutting them is stated; the decision and its owner are recorded; the reporting duty for a third-party incident is checked.

## Evidence

The request sent to the supplier, the access review notes and the decision log. The supplier register entry used during the exercise shows whether contacts and clauses were actually usable.

## Regulatory mapping

Evidences ICT third-party risk and continuity under DORA (DORA-11), the notification of incidents at a third-party service provider under the New York rule (NYDFS-500-17), and supply-chain aspects of continuity and crisis management under NIS2 (NIS2-21-2-C).

## Common pitfalls

No reachable contact outside business hours; contracts without a right to information or audit; and cutting access without knowing what stops, or never cutting it for fear of the impact. Make the supplier's answer slow and partial in the scenario: that is realistic.
