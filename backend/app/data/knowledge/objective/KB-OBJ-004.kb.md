# Prepare the first regulatory report before its deadline

## Objective

From the moment of awareness, the team drafts the first report to the competent authority (an early warning within 24 hours under NIS2, a notification within 72 hours under the UK NIS Regulations, an initial notification within 24 hours under CER) with the content the regulation requires, approved by the person accountable for notifications, and has it ready before the deadline on the scenario clock.

## Success criterion

The time of awareness is stated and recorded; the recipient authority and channel are identified; the draft contains the content the regulation requires; the draft is approved by the person accountable for notifications; the scenario clock shows it ready before the deadline.

## Evidence

The draft report, the approval record, and the decision log showing awareness, assessment and approval times. Keeping the draft is useful even when the scenario ends before submission: it becomes a template for the real thing.

## Regulatory mapping

Directly evidences the reporting duties of NIS2 Article 23 (NIS2-23), UK NIS Regulation 11 (UK-NIS-11) and CER Article 15 (CER-15). Where several regimes apply, the objective can require the table to decide which reports are due, which is itself a frequent gap.

## Common pitfalls

Waiting for certainty before notifying, when the first report is meant to be early and incomplete; not knowing the reporting portal or the credentials to use it; and nobody being designated to approve. An inject from the authority asking for news before the table has decided anything tests composure.
